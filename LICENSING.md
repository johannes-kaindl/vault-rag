# Licensing

Vault Retrieval is **free software under the AGPL-3.0-or-later** ([`LICENSE`](LICENSE)), and it
will stay that way. This file exists for the one case the AGPL does not cover: if its copyleft
does not fit your use case, a **commercial license is available on request**.

## Which license applies to what

| Part | License |
| --- | --- |
| Code | [AGPL-3.0-or-later](LICENSE) |
| Documentation & text | [CC BY-SA 4.0](LICENSE-DOCS) |

## Asking for a commercial license

Write to **code@jkaindl.de** and describe what you want to build. Useful to include: how the
plugin (or parts of it) would be distributed, and whether your product is itself open source.
There is no price list — small and non-commercial uses are usually fine under the AGPL anyway,
and it is worth asking before assuming otherwise.

## Why this is possible — and what would end it

Dual-licensing only works while **one party holds the rights to all of the code**. As of
2026-08-22 that is the case: every one of the 681 commits is authored by Johannes Kaindl.
(AI-assisted commits carry a `Co-Authored-By` trailer for transparency; they are work made
under the author's direction and do not create a second rights holder.)

**Contributions are accepted inbound under AGPL-3.0-or-later** — see
[`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md). That is deliberate: this project asks for **no CLA**,
because signing a rights transfer is a real hurdle for a drive-by fix, and drive-by fixes are
worth more here than a licensing option nobody has asked for yet.

The honest consequence: **once a third-party contribution is merged, a commercial license can
no longer be granted for the project as a whole** without that contributor's agreement. Should
that day come, the options are to ask them, to reimplement the part, or to drop the offer — and
this file gets updated to say which. Until then the offer above stands without asterisks.
